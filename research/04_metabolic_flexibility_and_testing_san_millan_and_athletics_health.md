## 1. Physiological Foundations: San-Millán & Brooks (2018)

Iñigo San-Millán and George Brooks formulated the modern biochemical interest in Zone 2 through their investigation of blood lactate accumulation and substrate oxidation:

* **Lactate as Metabolic Fuel:** Rather than a simple waste product, lactate is a primary metabolic substrate shuttled into slow-twitch (Type I) muscle fibers for oxidation via monocarboxylate transporters (MCT1) and mitochondrial lactate dehydrogenase (mLDH).
* **Metabolic Inflexibility:** Untrained subjects and individuals with metabolic syndrome exhibit premature reliance on glycolysis at low workloads, leading to early lactate accumulation and diminished fat oxidation.
* **Zone 2 Definition:** San-Millán defines Zone 2 as the highest workload at which blood lactate levels remain stable ($\approx \text{LT1}$, approximately 1.5–2.0 mmol/L in lab settings) and fat oxidation is near its peak ($\text{FatMax}$).

## 2. Critique of the Athletics & Health Institut (AHI) Perspective

The article by Marco Kaufmann (AHI München, 2026) exemplifies how physiological research is packaged in modern clinical and personal coaching environments:

### Strengths of the AHI Model:
1. **Accurate Critique of Universal Formulas:**  
   The article rightly demonstrates that population-level formulas like "220 - age" or Maffetone's "180 - age" are severely flawed for individual training control. They can easily misestimate an athlete's true metabolic threshold by 10–20 beats per minute.
2. **Diagnostic Precision:**  
   Advocating for spiroergometry (breath-gas analysis, $\text{VT1}/\text{VT2}$) or blood lactate testing provides an objective basis for physiological thresholds.
3. **Orthopedic & Injury Preservation:**  
   For recreational athletes with prior joint, tendon, or spinal complaints, the high-volume low-impact nature of slow aerobic running provides low injury rates.

### Scientific Limitations & Commercial Bias:
1. **Overstating Mitochondrial Exclusivity:**  
   The claim that Zone 2 is uniquely responsible for mitochondrial proliferation contradicts the 2025 meta-regression by Mølmen et al. and the review by Storoschuk et al. High-intensity and threshold work trigger equal or superior mitochondrial biogenesis signaling.
2. **Commercial Upselling:**  
   The article positions clinic-based spiroergometric testing as a mandatory prerequisite for productive aerobic training. In reality, field metrics (the classic "Talk Test", cardiac decoupling analysis $Pw:Hr$, and historical heart-rate distributions) provide sufficient accuracy for recreational progression without costly clinic visits.
3. **Transferability to Low-Volume Running:**  
   San-Millán's observational data is based heavily on professional cycling (e.g. UAE Team Emirates, Tadej Pogačar). Cycling involves zero eccentric muscle impact, allowing 4–6 hours of continuous Zone 2. In running, biomechanics and vertical impact forces at very low paces can degrade running cadence and ground contact mechanics.

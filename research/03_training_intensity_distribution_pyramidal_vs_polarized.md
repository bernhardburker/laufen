## 1. Comparing the Intensity Distribution Models

In sports science, endurance training is commonly modeled using a 3-zone framework based on ventilatory thresholds:
* **Zone 1 (Low / Aerobic Base):** Below the first lactate/ventilatory threshold ($\text{LT1} / \text{VT1}$).
* **Zone 2 (Moderate / Threshold):** Between $\text{LT1}$ and the second threshold ($\text{LT2} / \text{VT2} / \text{CP}$).
* **Zone 3 (High / Severe):** Above $\text{LT2}$ ($\ge \text{VO}_2\max$).

*(Note: In Garmin/Intervals.icu 7-zone models, Zone 1 maps to Z1/Z2, Zone 2 maps to Z3/Z4 Tempo/SubThreshold, and Zone 3 maps to Z5/Z6/Z7).*

```
Polarized TID (~80/10/10 or 80/0/20)        Pyramidal TID (~70/20/10)
┌──────────────┐                             ┌──────────────┐
│  Zone 3: 10% │                             │  Zone 3: 10% │
├──────────────┤                             ├──────────────┤
│  Zone 2: 10% │ (Minimizes Threshold)       │  Zone 2: 20% │ (Substantial Threshold)
├──────────────┴─────────────────────────┐   ├──────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
│              Zone 1: 80%               │   │              Zone 1: 70%               │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘   └────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Research Findings on Recreational vs. Elite Populations

### A. Real-World Amateur Behavior
Observational tracking of recreational half-marathon and marathon runners across multiple recent cohorts reveals that amateurs naturally and successfully operate under a **pyramidal distribution**:
* **Observed breakdown:** $\sim 68–72\%$ low intensity (Z1), $\sim 18–22\%$ moderate/threshold (Z2), and $\sim 10–14\%$ high intensity (Z3).
* Time spent in moderate-intensity running (steady-state tempo and cruise threshold intervals) correlates significantly with 10k, half-marathon, and marathon performance gains in sub-elite and recreational cohorts.

### B. Why Strict Polarized (80/20) Breaks Down at 3 Sessions/Week
1. **Mathematical Reality:**  
   If an athlete runs 3 sessions of 45 minutes (total 135 minutes/week), a strict 80/20 model dictates:
   * 108 minutes of Zone 1 / slow jog.
   * Only 27 minutes of total intensity per week.
   * Moderate threshold work is suppressed to near zero.
2. **Abundant Recovery Windows:**  
   Running 3 days per week provides **4 complete rest/recovery days** per week (48 to 72 hours between runs). The physiological rationale for avoiding Zone 2/3 (threshold fatigue accumulation) simply does not apply, as parasympathetic heart rate variability (HRV) and muscle glycogen fully recover within 48 hours.

### C. The Real Risk: Unstructured Monotony ("The Gray Rut")
The literature is unanimous on what actually harms recreational progress:
* It is **not** the inclusion of moderate/threshold work.
* It is **unstructured intensity monotony**: running every single session at a moderate "comfortably hard" pace without polar extremes (no true easy recovery runs and no true sharp intervals).

## 3. Structural Model for a 3-Day Adaptive Runner

The literature supports structuring 3 weekly runs around distinct physiological aims:

```
Run 1 (Weekly Anchor): Easy Aerobic Base (Zone 2)
  Duration: 45–60 min | Target: Capillarization, fat oxidation, recovery.
  Metric: HR-capped (< 158 bpm), zero pace pressure.

Run 2 (Metabolic Stimulus): Controlled Threshold / Cruise Intervals (Zone 3–4)
  Duration: 35–45 min | Target: Lactate clearance, cardiac stroke volume.
  Structure: 3x 8 min or 2x 12 min at sub-threshold (~165–174 bpm) with short jog recoveries.

Run 3 (Neuromuscular & VO2max): Short Intervals or Hill Strides (Zone 5)
  Duration: 30–35 min | Target: Mitochondrial signaling efficiency, cadence (> 165 spm).
  Structure: 8–10x 1 min fast / 1 min easy jog OR 6x 30s hill sprints.
```
